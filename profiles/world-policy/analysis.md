# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a public-affairs item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about politics, government action, international relations, or social policy.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Events that change the direction of a country or region or affect large populations for years: war and peace, regime change, landmark legislation or court rulings, major sanctions regimes, constitutional crises, sweeping social-policy reform.
- **7-8: Important.** Binding decisions or clear turning points: enacted laws with concrete effects, elections with a decided outcome, significant diplomatic agreements, budget or welfare changes affecting a whole sector, credible escalations in an armed conflict.
- **5-6: Useful.** Concrete, verifiable developments that help readers follow a political or policy story but have limited immediate breadth: committee votes, official reports, incremental regulatory steps, well-evidenced policy debate.
- **3-4: Low value.** Speculation, polling without a decision attached, procedural noise, partisan framing without new facts, commentary recycling known positions.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumor, unverified claims about motives, outrage bait, content with no attributable source or verifiable development.

# Evaluation guidance

Judge the magnitude and durability of the consequences, how many people are affected, whether the development is a decision or merely a statement, and how credible and independent the sourcing is. Separate reported facts from proposals, forecasts, allegations, and opinions, and say which is which. Distinguish an announced intention from an enacted measure. Do not reward an item for being dramatic, for matching one political side, or for volume of coverage alone; a small procedural step with large downstream effects can outrank a loud but inconsequential story.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
